BEFORE YOU BEGIN · THE DISTINCTION TO PROTECT
A founding client offer is not a discounted sale.
A discounted sale anchors your price downward — the customer buys cheap and resents the increase later. A founding client offer anchors the relationship upward — the customer becomes an insider with stake in your success. The framing is everything. This worksheet is calibrated to keep you on the right side of that distinction.

STEP THREE · THE OFFER

Build your founding client offer.

Four components. Skip nothing. Each one is doing structural work — leave any out and the offer slides toward "discounted sale" territory.

COMPONENT 01 · PRICE WITH A REASON
A price they're locking in — because they took the early risk.

Not "discounted." Permanent. Frame it as recognition of early relationship, not a markdown.

A 20–40% reduction from standard is typical. Less than 15% rarely feels meaningful; more than 50% starts to feel like a fire sale.

"Permanently, as long as the membership stays continuous"

"Because you're taking the early risk with us before we have a track record." Avoid the words discount, deal, or special offer — they reframe this as a markdown.

COMPONENT 02 · COHORT SIZE
A real number — and you hold to it.

Real scarcity, not manufactured urgency. The number has to be small enough to feel exclusive and large enough to actually fund the next phase.

Smaller (5–15) feels more exclusive but raises the per-customer ask. Larger (20–50) lowers the bar for any single yes but dilutes the insider feeling.

"First 15, then closed" — a real deadline, not a fake one.

COMPONENT 03 · INSIDER BENEFITS
What changes the relationship from buyer to participant.

Three specific things that don't cost you much but transform the experience. Not "premium support." Real access.

Common: a quarterly call with the owner · early access to new features · input into the roadmap · direct text/Slack channel to you · first priority on new products. Pick what's true to your business.

COMPONENT 04 · HONEST ROADMAP
Where you are. Where you're going. What their money funds.

Honesty about stage is what makes them an insider. Vagueness is what makes them suspicious.

"The core platform is live. Three of seven planned modules are complete."

"The remaining modules ship over the next 4 months — founding clients vote on the order."

"Twelve months of payroll for the development team finishing the platform." Be specific. Not "growth," not "operations" — what it actually buys.

STEP FOUR · THE CONVERSATION

The words you'll actually say.

Three moves. Each one needs your specifics — your relationship history, your honest stage description, your direct ask. The script you generate will use the offer you just built and customize each move with the language below.

MOVE 01 · RESTATE THE RELATIONSHIP
You're not opening a pitch. You're deepening a connection.

One sentence that says: "I'm coming to you specifically because of what's already between us." That sentence reframes the entire conversation.

"We've been talking on and off for six months about what I'm building, and you've given me real feedback every step."

MOVE 02 · DESCRIBE WITH SPECIFICITY
Concrete. Not a vision statement.

What they'll experience. What problem it solves for them, specifically. Where you are honestly. The stage disclosure isn't a weakness — it's what makes them an insider.

"It cuts the time your nurses spend on patient wellness check-ins from 40 minutes a week to 8."

"You'll get a 1-hour onboarding with me. Your team is on the platform within a week. You join the first founding-client roundtable in week three."

MOVE 03 · MAKE THE ASK DIRECTLY
No hedging. No artificial urgency.

"I'd like to know if this is a conversation worth having." Direct, unforced. The script will use your offer details from Step 3 to build this. Below is the one piece you need to add: how you want to leave the conversation.

"I'll send you the offer in writing by Friday and we'll talk Monday."
